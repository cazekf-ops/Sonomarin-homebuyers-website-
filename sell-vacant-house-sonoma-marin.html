A Vacant House Is a Liability, Not Just an Empty Building

Whether it came from an inheritance, a move you haven't finished, a rental between tenants, or a property that's simply sat unused, a vacant home isn't neutral — it's actively costing you money and carrying real risk every month it stays empty. Selling stops that clock.

We're Evan and Alex — local, licensed buyers based in Sonoma County. We buy vacant properties across both counties regularly and can move quickly since there's no tenant or resident to coordinate around.

The Real Cost of Letting a House Sit Empty

  • Vandalism and break-ins. Vacant properties are more likely targets, especially in more remote or rural parts of Sonoma and Marin County.
  • Squatters and unauthorized occupants. An empty home can attract unwanted occupants, which can become a serious legal and practical headache.
  • Insurance complications. Many standard homeowners policies limit or exclude coverage after a property has been vacant for 30-60 days, often requiring a separate, more expensive vacant home policy.
  • Undetected damage. A small leak or pest issue can turn into a major problem with no one there to catch it early.
  • Ongoing carrying costs. Property taxes, insurance, utilities, and general upkeep continue whether anyone's living there or not.
If your vacant property has squatters or unauthorized occupants, this is worth addressing sooner rather than later — resolving it can take time depending on the specifics. Reach out and we'll talk through your options honestly.

Vacant Property FAQs — Sonoma & Marin County

Why is it risky to leave a house vacant?

Vacant homes are more vulnerable to vandalism, break-ins, squatters, and undetected problems like burst pipes or roof leaks that can go unnoticed for months. They also continue to accrue property taxes, insurance, and maintenance costs with no one there to catch small issues before they become expensive ones.

Does a vacant house cost more to insure?

Often, yes. Many standard homeowners insurance policies limit or exclude coverage once a home has been vacant for a certain period (commonly 30-60 days), requiring a separate, typically more expensive vacant home policy to maintain coverage.

Can you buy a vacant house without me needing to be there?

Yes. We can evaluate and make an offer on a vacant property without requiring you to be present, and closing can typically be handled remotely through the title company if you're not local.

What if there are squatters or unauthorized occupants in the vacant house?

We've dealt with this situation before and can talk through your options. It's a serious issue that's best addressed sooner rather than later, since resolving it can take time — reach out and we'll walk through the specifics with you.

Do I need to secure or clean up the property before selling?

No. We buy vacant properties exactly as they are, including homes with deferred maintenance, damage, or items left behind. You don't need to board it up, clean it out, or make any repairs first.

How fast can you close on a vacant property?

As little as 7 days once the title is clear. Since there are no tenants or occupants to coordinate around, vacant properties are often among the fastest sales to complete.
