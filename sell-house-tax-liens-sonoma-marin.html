A Lien on the Property Doesn't Mean You Can't Sell

Discovering a lien on your home — whether it's for unpaid property taxes, an IRS debt, an unpaid contractor, or a court judgment — can feel like it closes off your options. It doesn't. Liens are routinely paid off from sale proceeds at closing, the same way a mortgage is. You don't need to resolve the lien yourself before you can sell.

We're Evan and Alex — local, licensed buyers based in Sonoma County. We've worked with homeowners facing all kinds of liens and can walk you through exactly how the payoff process works for your specific situation.

Property Tax Liens in California

When property taxes go unpaid, the county places a lien on the property for the amount owed, plus penalties and interest. In California, a property generally becomes "tax-defaulted" after taxes go unpaid, and the county tax collector can begin the process to sell it at public auction after five years of default for most residential property (the timeline can be shorter for certain nonresidential commercial properties). Until that point is reached, you retain ownership and full control — including the ability to sell.

The key takeaway: the longer taxes go unpaid, the more penalties and interest accumulate, eating into your equity. Selling sooner rather than later protects more of what you've built.

IRS and Federal Tax Liens

An IRS federal tax lien attaches to your property when back taxes are owed to the federal government. It doesn't prevent a sale, but it does need to be addressed — typically, the title company pays off the lien from your sale proceeds at closing to deliver clear title to the buyer.

Mechanic's Liens and Judgment Liens

If a contractor wasn't fully paid for work on the property, they may have filed a mechanic's lien. Similarly, an unpaid creditor who won a lawsuit against you may have recorded a judgment lien against your real estate. Both work the same way as tax liens for sale purposes: they're paid off from the proceeds at closing.

This page is general information, not legal or tax advice. Lien payoff amounts and priority can be complex, especially with multiple liens. Your county tax collector, the IRS, or a real estate attorney can confirm the specifics of your situation — and we're glad to point you toward local resources.

Tax Lien FAQs — Sonoma & Marin County

Can I sell my house if it has a property tax lien?

Yes. A property tax lien doesn't prevent a sale — the unpaid taxes, penalties, and interest are simply paid off from the sale proceeds at closing, and you keep whatever equity remains.

How long can I go without paying property taxes in California before losing the house?

In California, a property generally becomes tax-defaulted after taxes go unpaid, and the county tax collector can begin the process to sell it at public auction after five years of default (the timeline can be shorter for certain commercial property). Until that point, you retain ownership and can sell or pay off the balance at any time.

Does an IRS federal tax lien stop me from selling my house?

No, but it does need to be addressed at closing. An IRS lien attaches to your property when back taxes are owed, and the title company will typically pay it off from the proceeds to deliver clear title to the buyer, similar to how a mortgage payoff works.

What about a mechanic's lien or a judgment lien from a lawsuit?

Same principle applies. Liens from unpaid contractors (mechanic's liens) or court judgments are resolved from the sale proceeds at closing, the same way a mortgage or tax lien is handled. You don't need to pay them off yourself before selling.

What if I have more than one lien on the property?

Multiple liens are common and can usually all be resolved at closing, paid out in the order required by law from the sale proceeds. The title company handles this process to make sure everything is cleared properly.

How fast can you close if I have a lien on my property?

As little as 7 days once the lien payoff amounts are confirmed with the relevant agency or creditor, or on whatever timeline works for your situation.
