Behind on Payments? The Earlier You Act, the More Options You Have

Falling behind on your mortgage, property taxes, or HOA dues is stressful, but it's rarely an emergency on day one — it becomes one the longer it's left unaddressed. Whether it's a job loss, medical bill, or just a rough stretch, the good news is that acting early gives you far more control over the outcome than waiting does.

We're Evan and Alex — local, licensed buyers based in Sonoma County. We've helped homeowners across both counties get ahead of financial hardship before it turns into something bigger, like foreclosure or a tax sale. No judgment — just honest options.

Behind on Property Taxes or HOA Dues?

It's not just mortgage payments that can spiral. Unpaid property taxes can eventually lead to a tax lien and, if left long enough, a tax sale. Unpaid HOA dues can result in liens as well. In every case, the same principle applies: selling before the debt escalates lets you pay it off from the proceeds and keep what's left, rather than losing equity to penalties, interest, or a forced sale down the line.

Your Options When You're Behind

  • Catch up the payments — if the hardship was temporary and you can realistically get current.
  • Negotiate a payment plan — with your lender, the county tax collector, or your HOA, depending on what's behind.
  • Sell before it escalates — pay off what's owed from the proceeds and keep your equity. This is where we come in.
  • List it on the market with us — if you have time and it would net more, we're licensed agents and will tell you honestly.

This page is general information, not financial or legal advice. If you're not sure how far behind you are or what your specific timeline looks like, your lender, the county tax collector, or a HUD-approved housing counselor can clarify your exact situation — often for free.

Common Questions

Behind on Payments FAQs — Sonoma & Marin County

How far behind on payments do I need to be before selling makes sense?

There's no set threshold — the sooner you act, the more options and equity you protect. Even one or two missed payments is a good time to explore selling, before late fees and interest accumulate further or the situation moves toward formal foreclosure.

I've received a Notice of Default. Is this the right page for me?

If you've already received a formal Notice of Default, our dedicated foreclosure page covers the California foreclosure timeline and your options in more detail. This page is for homeowners who are behind on payments but haven't yet reached that stage.

Can you help if I'm behind on property taxes, not my mortgage?

Yes. Unpaid property taxes can eventually lead to a tax lien or, if left long enough, a tax sale. Selling before that happens lets you pay off what's owed and keep the remaining equity, the same as with a mortgage default.

What if I have unpaid HOA dues or other liens on the property?

That's fine — outstanding HOA dues, tax liens, and similar debts are simply settled from the sale proceeds at closing, the same way a mortgage payoff works. You don't need to resolve them yourself beforehand.

Will selling stop collection calls and protect my credit?

Paying off the debt through a sale resolves the underlying obligation, which stops further collection activity tied to that debt and avoids the more serious credit damage that comes with a completed foreclosure or tax sale.

How fast can you close?

As little as 7 days once the title is clear, or on whatever timeline works for you. Acting earlier gives us more room to move at a comfortable pace rather than racing a deadline.
