Done Being a Landlord? We Make the Exit Simple.

Owning a rental sounds simple until it isn't — late rent, property damage, a tenant who won't answer calls, or just the accumulated exhaustion of managing a property you no longer want. Whatever the reason, you don't need to fix the tenant situation before you can sell. We buy rental properties as-is, tenants and all.

We're Evan and Alex — local, licensed buyers based in Sonoma County. We've purchased occupied rentals across both counties and understand the specific challenges landlords face here. No judgment, no pressure — just a straightforward path out.

You Don't Have to Evict Before You Sell

This is the biggest misconception we hear from landlords: that tenants need to be out before a sale can happen. In most cases, that's not true. We can purchase the property with the tenancy in place, which means:

  • No eviction filing, court dates, or legal fees on your end
  • No months-long wait for an eviction process to conclude
  • No confrontation with tenants you'd rather not deal with directly

If tenants ultimately need to be relocated, that becomes part of our process after closing — not a hurdle you have to clear first.

Common Landlord Situations We Help With

  • Tenants behind on rent — you don't need to collect what's owed before selling
  • Property damage from tenants — we factor actual condition into the offer, no repairs needed from you
  • Difficult or unresponsive tenants — we handle the situation, not you
  • Long-distance or inherited rentals — managing from afar (or managing a property you never wanted) gets old fast
  • Multiple rentals and burnout — sometimes the simplest fix is exiting the business entirely

A note on California tenant law. California has strong tenant protections (including statewide just-cause eviction rules under AB 1482 in many cases), and eviction can be a slow, costly process even when you're in the right. Selling with tenants in place often sidesteps that entirely — it's frequently the fastest, least stressful option available.

What About the Lease?

An existing lease doesn't block a sale. Under California law, a new owner generally takes the property subject to the current lease, meaning the tenancy can simply continue under new ownership. Depending on your specific lease and goals, there may be other options too — we'll walk through what applies to your situation.

Common Questions

Problem Tenants FAQs — Sonoma & Marin County

Can I sell my rental property while tenants are still living there?

Yes. We regularly buy occupied rental properties in Sonoma and Marin County. You don't need to evict tenants or wait for a lease to end before selling — we can purchase the property with tenants in place.

Do I need to evict my tenants before selling?

No. In most cases we can buy the property as-is with the existing tenancy, which saves you the time, cost, and legal complexity of an eviction. If the tenants need to be relocated after the sale, that becomes our process to manage, not yours.

What if my tenants are behind on rent or have damaged the property?

That's exactly the kind of situation we help with. Unpaid rent and tenant-caused damage are common reasons landlords want out — we factor the property's actual condition into our offer, so you don't need to collect back rent or repair damage before selling.

Do I have to pay for "cash for keys" or relocation assistance?

Not necessarily. Depending on your situation, we can often handle tenant relocation as part of the purchase rather than requiring you to negotiate and pay for it yourself beforehand.

What if my tenants have a long-term lease?

An existing lease doesn't prevent a sale. In California, a new owner generally takes the property subject to the existing lease, so the tenancy can simply continue under new ownership — or we can work with you on other options depending on the lease terms.

How fast can you close on an occupied rental?

As little as 7 days once the title is clear, or on whatever timeline fits your situation. We're comfortable moving at whatever pace works given the tenancy.
